# It Works — Product Vision Insights: The Five-Year Habit

Recorded: 2026-11-02. These are product-owner insights and vision statements, not instructions to coding agents or a commitment to build every feature now.

## 1. Upgrade an existing habit, not create a new one
The product owner has kept a personal iPhone note titled **It Works** readily accessible for **more than five years**. It holds personal vision, imagery, wishes and aspirations. This is evidence of lasting personal demand for that kind of space — **not yet evidence that a new app will be adopted**.

**Product implication:** Preserve what makes an immediately accessible, free-form, familiar note valuable. Do not impose a complicated workflow to gain structure.

## 2. Native iPhone experience is critical to the long-term outcome
Earlier browser prototypes did not replace the long-standing note because they offered insufficient added everyday value.

Desired capabilities:
- Personally scheduled notifications and reminders.
- Home Screen widgets.
- Near-instant access and editing.
- Reliable, trustworthy persistence.
- An app that can proactively reach the user when explicitly configured, rather than being entirely passive.

**Sequencing nuance:** The earlier decision, **web/PWA first, native-ready**, remains the initial development approach. However, **native iPhone experience is a key long-term product success requirement**, not an unimportant optional decoration. Browser-only success is insufficient if it fails to replace the current daily habit.

## 3. Life Dashboard is the core
A user defines their own life domains/folders, indicators, standards, goals and desired outcomes.

**Current state (LEFT) ↔ Personal standard / goal (RIGHT)**

The system does not reinterpret the user's desires or impose outside criteria for success. The dashboard is primarily a clear holistic depiction of the user's life, not an advice machine.

## 4. Personal and expressive rather than impersonal productivity
The experience must preserve the freedom of a personal note:
- Free-form text.
- Images, personal Vision Board and Purpose.
- Easy entry, editing and reordering of goals and areas.
- An attractive, emotionally resonant space worth opening voluntarily.

Do not let rigid charts and performance tracking displace the user's personal vision.

## 5. Prior Success OS is an inspiration library, not mandatory scope
Earlier Success OS concepts included:
- Mission Dashboard.
- Habit Tracker.
- Weekly Review.
- Momentum System.
- Strategic Coaching.

These are optional sources of ideas. **First deliver the vision/current-versus-standards experience.** Habit routines, reviews and coaching are secondary.

## 6. North-star product success criterion
> I voluntarily prefer It Works to the iPhone note I have used for five years.

This matters more initially than feature count or technical sophistication.

A successful replacement **retains the strengths of the existing note** and adds tangible utility without new friction. A product that is functionally impressive but not chosen for daily life has not yet succeeded.

## Central vision
**It Works = My Personal Life Operating System.**

A personal, beautiful, continually accessible space on the iPhone where I see the life I desire, the life I have now and my actual progress — on my own terms.

**Product philosophy:** Upgrade an existing five-year habit; don't replace it with a complicated new workflow.

## Implications for future evaluation
- Evaluate **time-to-open, time-to-edit, friction and spontaneous return**, not merely feature checklist completion.
- Ensure notes and pictures can coexist with structured metrics and standards.
- Observe whether the user *actually switches* from the old note in sustained daily use.
- Maintain a clear distinction between **prototype functionality** and the eventual **native experience** (notifications/widgets and reliable multi-device persistence).
- The final dashboard look remains a separate discussion; Apple Health is a possible inspiration, not a fixed visual specification.
